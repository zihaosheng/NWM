01 / THE PROBLEM

A safe ending
can be a wrong prediction.

Most driving datasets record interactions that ended safely. World models trained on them can inherit an optimistic view of what happens next.

When a collision is imminent, a model may imagine another vehicle disappearing, the ego vehicle stopping, or the scene freezing. These futures can look plausible while hiding the consequences of a dangerous action.

We introduce the Neutral World Model (NWM): a controllable video diffusion model trained on both routine driving and real accident videos, with a bounding-box-guided objective that emphasizes surrounding road users.

↳

The goal is faithful imagination of both safe and dangerous outcomes.

02 / THE APPROACH

Learn both kinds of future.
Imagine before choosing.

A single framework connects future generation,
spatial control, and action selection.

01

Broaden the experience

Combine nuScenes routine driving with MM-AU real accident videos to learn the outcomes underrepresented in conventional driving data.

02

Focus on road users

A bounding-box-guided loss gives additional weight to agent regions while preserving the base video-generation objective.

03

Evaluate imagined actions

Predict a future for each candidate ego trajectory, score safety, progress, and comfort, and select the highest-reward action.

PRESERVING ROUTINE-DRIVING FIDELITY

Recognize danger.
Keep safe futures safe.

NWM’s false collision rate is 8% on 50 collision-free MM-AU clips, compared with its 86% recall on imminent accident scenes in the same visual domain.

On 100 routine nuScenes clips, no evaluated model exceeds a 1% false collision rate. NWM is comparable to or better than Vista and GEM across FID, FVD, and JEDi on both datasets.

IN THE AUTHORS’ WORDS

Abstract

World models allow autonomous vehicles to imagine the consequences of candidate actions before execution, but their reliability depends on the futures represented in their training data. We identify a survivorship bias in driving world models: because the futures recorded in prevailing driving datasets almost always end safely, existing models often imagine benign outcomes even when a collision is imminent. We introduce a neutral world model (NWM) that learns both routine and safety-critical futures from a mixture of routine driving and real accident videos with a bounding-box-guided objective. The NWM more faithfully anticipates imminent collisions, while maintaining a low false collision rate and competitive generation quality in routine driving. We further use the model as an imagination engine for decision-making. Given multiple ego trajectories, the NWM predicts the future associated with each candidate, and a reward function evaluates these imagined outcomes before selecting an action. On challenging NAVSIM scenarios, this procedure selects the better trajectory more reliably than the baseline world model. Zero-shot experiments with real-world pedestrian dummy crossing data collected using an autonomous research vehicle further demonstrate transfer to unseen sensor observations. These results show that correcting survivorship bias enables world models to more faithfully reason about futures and support safety-critical decision-making in autonomous driving.
